Focus listening is defined by what it *does not* do. Music for studying should not
compete with the inner voice a reader uses to process text, so lyric-heavy tracks in
the listener's native language interrupt comprehension more than instrumental tracks
at the same volume. Instrumental or non-native-language vocals are the safer default.

Target audio features: energy between 0.25 and 0.45, tempo between 60 and 90 BPM, and
moderate-to-high acousticness. Sharp dynamic swings pull attention, so a flat dynamic
range beats a dramatic one. Lofi, ambient, and slow instrumental jazz all satisfy this.

Explicit lyrics are usually undesirable here, both because focus sessions often happen
in shared spaces (libraries, offices, classrooms) and because emotionally charged
language is itself an attention cost.
